# Craft comparison

## Crafting display rules

These user requirements were reaffirmed on October 17 after repeated display regressions. Keep them as explicit regression checks when changing recognition, scoring, strategy comparisons or styles:

1. Keep the best current stat candidate visible, even when its first reading is uncertain. A weaker retry must not replace it with a blank or a weaker alternate. Stronger evidence may replace its name or percentage within the same encounter; a genuine new artifact or capture context starts fresh.
2. If the candidate name and signed percentage are available, show its provisional weighted score, such as `~20`, rather than a dash. Preserve uncertainty and raw evidence; a display preview does not become a verified row or enter a confirmed subtotal merely by being visible.
3. The main score box is **red when its displayed score or subtotal is lower than its benchmark**, including uncertain, inferred and provisional points. Multi-strategy rendering must not overwrite that red state with amber merely because those points need review. With multiple selected strategies, every required comparison must have a numeric benchmark and be lower; an open slot or unavailable benchmark is not a loss.
4. Keep capture completeness, confidence, Keep eligibility and the three-unlocked-stat Keep reason separate from score colour. The Keep reason remains visible but does not hide a lower score's red background.

Regression coverage: `scripts/test_scanner_recognition.js` checks candidate retention and stronger replacement; `scripts/test_scanner_comparison.js` checks preview scoring without promoting raw evidence; `scripts/test_strategy_scanner.js` checks provisional numeric comparisons; the real browser scenarios `--glance` and `--provisional-colours` check the displayed stat rows, CSS colours and multi-strategy rendering. Run them through `scripts/run_scanner_ocr_fixture.js`. The general glance test disables strategy comparisons, so both browser scenarios are required. `ui/AGENTS.md` points future editing agents to this contract.

## Strategy comparison

Profiles now share one owned artifact inventory across independent army strategies. Army manages troop choices, duplicates, favorites, ordering and archived strategies; switching or saving never rewrites shared perk ratings. Each strategy has its own rating overrides and museum layout. The initial unconfigured strategy retains broad factory scoring; configured strategies exclude unselected offensive troop perks, retain defensive/general preferences, and include linked troop families. Explicit strategy overrides, including zero, take precedence. Migrated current armies preserve their previous effective ratings in a legacy snapshot; other converted presets start from clean army defaults. Global/profile inheritance is an explicit choice for migrated strategies because historical army-generated zeros cannot be distinguished from manual preferences. Original storage is retained in a profile migration backup, and full planner backups include strategies and layouts.

The active strategy controls detailed artifact scores and the displayed museum layout. The comparison selection is independent and defaults to all non-archived strategies, including newly added strategies until a subset is selected. Priority shows selectable strategy score columns; Collection adds per-strategy score/rank columns, coverage filters and A/B breakdowns; Active Artifact Totals sums actual unlocked percentages from the selected strategy's layout. Perk ratings exposes local overrides and their inherited sources. Both scanner pages use the same rating resolver. Council priority, Council ratings and Council lineups retain their separate profile-based stat system, with their scope labeled on each page.

Crafting compares each captured item against the two best owned copies of the same hall/category **for every selected strategy**, including unused artifacts and artifacts referenced by other strategy layouts. War Equipment retains four museum slots while crafting uses a two-item collection target. The strategy table separates maximum upgraded score, reroll potential, best owned, second owned, ties, open slots and improvements. Unknown or conflicting fields, incomplete owned stats and uncertain rerolls remain review states; rejection requires complete reliable comparisons for every included strategy. A tie does not displace an owned copy. Keeping saves one shared physical artifact, and updates all strategy benchmarks. Changing strategy while scanning updates scores without reloading or losing the capture. Benchmarks are cached independently per rating resolver/revision and rebuilt when owned data changes.

Strategy verification: `node scripts/test_strategies.js`, `node scripts/test_strategy_scanner.js`, `node scripts/test_army_unused_ratings.js`, `node scripts/test_perk_groups.js`, `node scripts/test_strategy_management_ui.js --summary`, `node scripts/test_strategy_collection_ui.js --summary`, and `node scripts/run_strategy_browser_check.js --summary`. Browser scenarios use deterministic recognition to check page behavior; they do not establish live OCR accuracy.

The capture bar reports identified fields, including direct reads, inferred values and explicit manual entries, with a separate count for each source. An ordinary craft has 17 required fields: five names, five percentages, five star states, category and hall. Any remaining unknown field keeps the bar amber with a manual-entry link that opens and focuses its correction control; Keep stays disabled until the item is complete. Editing one field preserves the other fields' provenance. A remembered hall or category does not identify a new craft. Verify at mobile or desktop sizes with `node scripts/run_scanner_ocr_fixture.js --identification --viewport=480x640 --summary`.

October 17 Keep action: both the main button and the item log save a complete five-stat craft directly to the active profile's Collection, including its recognized name, artwork, level-one ranges, star states and reroll eligibility. Benchmarks update immediately. Previously kept crafts in pending inventory move to their original profiles' collections when browser storage opens. Separate physical crafts retain distinct IDs even with identical stats; repeated clicks cannot duplicate one encounter. Both buttons accept complete held rows with inferred values while preserving their provenance. Missing category, incomplete or conflicting rows, paused capture and a changed profile disable the main button with a visible explanation. Failed saves remain retryable. The local item log retains names and artwork across reopening; downloaded JSON retains names and other text metadata but omits images. Older logs cannot recover artwork or names that were never recorded. Verify with `node scripts/run_scanner_ocr_fixture.js --crafting-keep --summary` (also supports `--viewport=480x640`).

The October 16 performance review caches owned category cutoffs for a stable collection and rating resolver, and memoizes active rating lookups across preview updates. A new collection snapshot or rating resolver invalidates the cutoffs; category, Hall, axes and capacity still select their appropriate benchmark. Focus/storage changes refresh profile/army/global ratings. The shared scorer and maximum/reroll rules are unchanged. A synthetic 500-item collection measured 11.10 ms per repeated comparison before caching and 0.049 ms after caching (225x), excluding OCR/capture. See `reports/scanner-comparison-performance.json`.

Assumed names, inferred percentages, numeric/name disagreements and tier conflicts do not enter crafting comparison totals until verified. Partial reads show only their readable subtotal and cannot produce a completed top-two decision.

The crafting scanner displays its existing Offensive and Defensive scores plus a separate comparison with the active profile's owned collection. Hall and Base Type (Off/Def) toggles at the top right of both scanner pages use the existing shared per-profile primary-filter preferences from Priority, Collection and Council. They update scanner recognition/scoring controls, restore across navigation, and resync on focus. Storage recognition updates the shared Hall when it identifies a different hall.

Crafting category detection reads the coloured label above the benefits panel from the full shared-source preview, separately from the unchanged perk crop. It selects Weapon, Armor, Jewelry, Pottery or Equipment automatically, displays the detected category, uses it for comparison, and includes it in the saved item log. Pottery/Jewelry identify Main and Equipment identifies War; a War prefix also identifies War. Weapon and Armor otherwise use the selected hall. The source must include the category label even when only the benefits panel is calibrated. Missing, ambiguous or very weak labels remain unread; a new unread item cannot inherit the previous auto-detected category. A category override is available inside Correct Recognition, and category preferences are shared with Storage Scanner per profile/hall. Legendary/Mythical do not replace a crafted category preference.

The category OCR uses an exact category whitelist without fuzzy guessing. Clear category words need confidence 70; a complete single category label in an ornate font can be accepted at confidence 40. The existing Pottery crafting screenshot passes real OCR detection, all five perk values still match, and Storage Scanner regression checks pass. Other categories have deterministic parser coverage but still need representative live screenshots to establish their OCR accuracy. Category recognition canvases remain temporary; item artwork is retained locally in the log and kept collection records, with no uploads. The comparison includes equipped and Unused owned items, and does not treat unkept crafting logs or ordinary pending inventory as ownership.

The top group size comes from the same slot configuration used by Collection: Main Weapon, Armor, Jewelry and Pottery each have two places; War Weapon and Armor have two, and War Equipment has four. A confirmed craft receives **BETTER THAN YOUR BEST N** when its score strictly exceeds the Nth-highest owned score. This means it enters the top group, rather than beating every member. The scanner also shows its rank, cutoff and difference. Equal scores do not displace an existing item.

A current craft with three detected unlocked stats always receives a green KEEP with a prominent **★★★ 3 STATS UNLOCKED · AUTO KEEP** label. This overrides low scores, provisional or unreadable perks, pending stat corrections, missing category selection, and unavailable collection data. The automatic rule uses the detected star count rather than inventing missing stars; unclear or fewer stars use the normal scoring decision. It stays steady during rereads, and clears on reset or pause. Numerical scoring remains available independently.

Crafted items always sum all five fully upgraded stats on both sides of the comparison; a saved Showcase preference from another page cannot select just one line. The header shows factual upgraded Offensive and Defensive sums plus Total. Each stat row also shows its individual combined score. Both sides use the active profile's current ratings and army-aware defaults. The comparison adds one ideal reroll of the weakest eligible stat, using the highest legal benefit score for that category and strategy. Fresh crafts have all five stats eligible; owned scans use their captured or manually confirmed dice mask. Unknown eligibility receives no guaranteed bonus and makes affected comparisons provisional. Locked rows and duplicate perks count. Storage Scanner retains its Hall/Showcase options. See [artifact reroll behavior](artifact-rerolls.md).

With fewer than N owned items the scanner reports open places instead of claiming improvement over an established cutoff. Incomplete owned stats, failed collection loading, no selected axis, or unconfirmed/stale crafting results prevent a comparison flag. Retry, correction and capture lifecycle changes update it. Refocusing the page reloads the active profile's collection; storage events also trigger a reload. No collection assignments or stored crafting logs are changed by the comparison.

The pinned summary shows large Offensive, Defensive and comparison scores with a large decision word. Green KEEP means a confirmed craft enters the top group or fills an open place. Red PASS means it ties or falls below the cutoff. Amber CHECK/WAIT means the decision is unavailable. Detailed stat rows, setup and logs remain below the summary, which stays visible while scrolling. The narrow layout uses an icon rail so navigation does not push the scores out of view.

Scores and the comparison signal stay steady during rereads and image transitions, replacing the last display only when a new scoreable result is available. LAST READ · Updating identifies the retained read. Same-profile background collection reloads keep the existing cutoff visible until the reload completes. Capture pauses, pending corrections, and unscoreable completed reads retain the last numbers with a status label but show CHECK rather than retaining a keep recommendation. Stop, new capture sessions, and recognition-setting changes clear the retained score. A failed collection reload also removes the recommendation.

Readable stat names and percentages contribute their own scores even when fewer than five rows are complete. Such results are labeled **subtotal**, show the readable-row count, and stay amber unless three open stats trigger the keeper exception. Missing, unknown, fabricated or conflicting rows contribute no score and cannot receive reroll gain; their original positions are preserved. No readable rows means no score. Missing names or percentages are never replaced with a highest-rated perk or a top-tier percentage. Five readable stats with other OCR warnings can still receive a provisional total. Missing reroll eligibility is unknown rather than implicitly true in the scoring adapter, so it cannot create a default reroll gain. Recognised or manually specified eligibility still contributes the best single reroll gain. Raw OCR and saved observations are never replaced by the scoring view.

Real headless Chrome layout checks passed at 480×640, 640×720, 960×720 and 1440×900, including expanded setup, pinned visibility after scrolling, and optimistic decisions. Run `node scripts/run_scanner_ocr_fixture.js --glance --viewport=480x640 --report --summary` to repeat a viewport and save its screenshot/report. These checks use synthetic OCR responses and cannot establish human peripheral perception or live OCR accuracy.

Uncertain crafting rows keep their best current candidate through weaker retries, including when the first read was already suggested or disputed. A stronger candidate can replace it within the same encounter. The stat row shows a provisional `~` score when its name and signed percentage are available; that preview remains excluded from confirmed totals, reroll gains and capture completeness. Missing names or percentages still require evidence.

## Speed review, October 17, 2026

The one-third latency target is **not met overall**. Controlled before/after runs measured the first readable sample through complete capture and comparison availability, including production controller, scoring, real collector serialization/rendering and two animation frames. Each side completed all 24 cases with exact names, signed values, category, detected Hall and five star states. All 24 paired accuracy/completion signatures match. The two fixtures are the original `pic1` and independently labelled recording frame `video161`; `pic1` Hall and star labels were manually checked against its original pixels. Fresh-craft dice eligibility remains rule-derived rather than independently pixel-labelled.

The controlled snapshots retain the original comparison/rating behavior to isolate speed changes from concurrent strategy work. The difficult `video174` pilot retained exact values with conflicting or inferred evidence and only 76% capture after 12 seconds; it is excluded from successful full-capture percentiles and remains a failed complete-capture case.

The six reviewed steps have these decisions:

1. Keep the reproducible full-controller benchmark, explicit cold/warm preparation, per-fixture p50/p95 and separate failed/partial cases. Source hashes and individual cases are saved in the reports.
2. Keep exact crop reuse only within the same scoped encounter, profile, geometry and worker mode. Strong compatible fields can be reused; changed pixels, ambiguous fields, category and Hall continue to receive fresh evidence. Fifteen real OCR paired cases measured 3285.0 → 1178.7 ms in total, with 30 → 15 OCR calls. The changed-byte and new-encounter cases verify selective invalidation. This measures repeat crops, not a new live artifact.
3. Reject an additional production OCR worker. The 40-pair experiment preserved results but warm p50 changed 203.3 → 206.6 ms, p95 464.7 → 463.0 ms; the small total-time gain does not justify added startup, memory and lifecycle complexity. Production keeps its existing worker arrangement.
4. Keep one atomic logging batch per read, incremental item DOM updates, encounter indexes and unchanged-save suppression. Full observation history and existing export schema remain available; persistence submission stays immediate and IndexedDB completion remains asynchronous. The JSON reports include collector-call diagnostics: the candidate atomic call also contains publication/scoring, while baseline collector calls exclude that work, so these durations are overlapping diagnostic scopes rather than an isolated logging comparison. Both end-to-end measurements include all work through paint.
5. Keep same-size frame buffers and write median pixels into an existing input buffer. Exact RGBA and transparent-frame replacement tests pass. A separate 960×540 Node median-loop microbenchmark measured 12.43 → 10.93 ms (12.0%), avoiding 2,073,600 bytes per snapshot; this excludes canvas readback and OCR.
6. Keep the existing 100 ms sampling cadence and 220 ms crafting settling gate. The evidence does not support trading temporal stability for a shorter delay.
