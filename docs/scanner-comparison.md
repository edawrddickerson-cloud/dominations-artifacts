# Craft comparison

## Crafting display rules

These user requirements were reaffirmed on October 17 after repeated display regressions. Keep them as explicit regression checks when changing recognition, scoring, strategy comparisons or styles:

1. Keep the best current stat candidate visible, even when its first reading is uncertain. A weaker retry must not replace it with a blank or a weaker alternate. Stronger evidence may replace its name or percentage within the same encounter; a genuine new artifact or capture context starts fresh.
2. If the candidate name and signed percentage are available, show its provisional weighted score, such as `~20`, rather than a dash. Preserve uncertainty and raw evidence; a display preview does not become a verified row or enter a confirmed subtotal merely by being visible.
3. The main score box is **green when its displayed score or subtotal meets or exceeds its numeric benchmark**, including uncertain, inferred and provisional points (for example, 180 against 170). Review indicators and confirmed Keep eligibility stay separate. It is **red when its displayed score or subtotal is lower than its benchmark**, except for the three-unlocked-stat Keep override in rule 4. Multi-strategy rendering must preserve these numeric colours even when points need review. A numeric win or tie for the visible active strategy or any selected strategy is green; red requires every required selected comparison to have a numeric benchmark and be lower. An open slot or unavailable benchmark is not a loss. A green tie does not claim a replacement.
4. Exactly three unlocked stats always make the main box **green**, regardless of score versus benchmark, missing percentages or category, or multi-strategy results. Show **Keep ? 3 stats unlocked** in bold. This explicit Keep override takes precedence over red and amber score states. Other capture completeness, confidence and Keep eligibility states remain separate from score colour. Clear the override for the next item unless it also has exactly three unlocked stats.
5. Keep the capture bar compact: its amber identified count is sufficient while fields are missing; do not add a manual-entry warning line that changes its height. Within the same artifact, keep identified stats, star states, category and hall provenance through weaker retries. A completed 17/17 read stays complete; stronger stat evidence may update its result. A genuine new artifact or capture context starts fresh.

Regression coverage: `scripts/test_scanner_recognition.js` checks candidate retention and stronger replacement; `scripts/test_scanner_comparison.js` checks preview scoring without promoting raw evidence; `scripts/test_strategy_scanner.js` checks provisional numeric comparisons; the real browser scenarios `--glance` and `--provisional-colours` check the displayed stat rows, CSS colours and multi-strategy rendering. Run them through `scripts/run_scanner_ocr_fixture.js`. The general glance test disables strategy comparisons, so both browser scenarios are required. `ui/AGENTS.md` points future editing agents to this contract.

## Selected crafting purposes

The user-facing name is Collection targets throughout the editor, scanner and settings links. Existing purpose storage keys, IDs and APIs remain compatible. The full stat catalogue, global/group defaults and strategy columns live inside Advanced ratings, collapsed by default. The gold Add target button sits directly beneath the saved-goal list. Column headings wrap within their shaded table cells, including on narrow screens.

The Crafting purposes workspace saves user-named targets, such as General defence or Anti bomber. Each target can combine any number of selections from Attacking, Defensive and Economy. The three tabs choose components of the current draft; saved targets have separate Track checkboxes for scanner comparison. Save purpose commits its name, selections and individual stat exclusions; shared rating changes still save immediately. The configuration panel on the right (stacked below on small screens) is one union of all draft selections across tabs: every canonical stat appears once even if several selected units use it. Users can include or exclude individual stats without changing their shared rating. Each section lists a protected default saved purpose first: Offensive - General, Defensive - General, or Economy - General. These fixed names cannot be changed and these purposes cannot be deleted. They start with every catalogue stat in their category, including zero-rated stats, while individual stat inclusion remains editable. General Purpose is no longer a source checkbox; earlier general-* selections remain supported internally for saved-goal compatibility. Observed artifact-type filters affect the displayed list only; incomplete type evidence is not treated as proof that other stats are unavailable. The Army tab and navigation entry are removed; army.html redirects to ratings.html#crafting-purposes, and the default landing page is ratings.html. The redundant Broader purposes presets remain removed.

Perk ratings also offers Group by → Crafting purpose. It renders every selected purpose as a compact section with its 0–10 perk ratings, using the same controls as the top configuration panel. Search matches purpose names or included perks. These controls directly edit the same profile Off/Def ratings as the older section: edits in either place update all overlapping purposes and the scanner. Global ratings and explicit loadout overrides remain separate.

Creating a purpose requires a unique user title and at least one included stat, and enables tracking by default. Editing or renaming retains its stable ID. The right-hand list supports stat search and observed artifact-type filtering within the header's selected Hall. Name, category selections and per-stat inclusion changes are drafts until Save purpose; tab changes, filters and shared-rating refreshes retain that draft. Saved definitions, tracking flags, active editor ID and migration backups persist in the profile settings record. Membership changes invalidate owned benchmark caches. Removing a stat or deleting a purpose leaves shared stat ratings intact. Saved purposes are isolated between profiles.

Players can track any number of saved named purposes. The scanner scores their combined included stat sets, rather than counting individual unit/category selections as separate purposes. Unit attack/counter selections use canonical unit scopes and existing unit links; explicit aircraft membership also includes helicopters. Air defence buildings support aircraft counters. Each useful line starts with its maximum upgraded magnitude, including locked lines, without hypothetical rerolls. Deduplicating selection definitions never removes repeated physical stat lines on an artifact: each real line still contributes once. Damage and HP points are a selection score, not a combined combat percentage.

Purpose ratings use a fixed axis per stat. Invading perks, defensive buildings, defensive towers, defenders and Looted Resource Refund use Def; other perks, including resource looting, production and research, use Off. Every purpose uses the same mapping. Each field identifies its axis and edits the corresponding shared profile rating. Clearing restores global/group/factory inheritance; zero ignores a perk. Purpose controls and scoring use the nonnegative 0–10 range; negative values entered in the older editor are treated as ignored for purpose scoring without rewriting the stored value. Both page and scanner use canonical factory defaults. Schema v4 retains previous selection records in legacySelections, combines old enabled built-in selections into one named My crafting purpose, preserves custom names and stat membership, and imports configured armies as named attacking targets (tracking the active army by default). The existing army data and explicit strategy overrides remain intact. Older independent weights retain the schema-v3 migration behavior: legacyPurposeRatings backs them up, existing explicit profile values win, and the first selected purpose wins remaining conflicts. Migration is idempotent and writes its schema marker before emitting shared-rating events. Global values and strategy-specific overrides are not rewritten. Benchmark cache keys include the named target's combined included stat set and effective shared ratings.

With purposes selected, the large box shows the number of matching purposes and lists every selected purpose, including failures, with its weighted score and useful line count. A match must have a positive score and strictly exceed that purpose's weighted second-best owned artifact of the same Hall and type, or fill an open top-two place. There is no additional quality threshold; an irrelevant or fully ignored craft cannot fill a vacancy. Equipped and Unused copies both count; duplicate IDs count once. Ties retain existing artifacts. Incomplete owned potential blocks an improvement claim. Inferred/partial numeric matches retain review indicators and do not change the Keep button's separate identification checks.

In this opt-in mode, any purpose hit makes the box green; no hits make it red when comparisons are available, or amber while waiting for data. Strategy comparisons remain below. The three-unlocked-stats Keep override stays green regardless of purpose results. With no purposes selected, the existing strategy-based numeric colour rules apply. Reset, pause, collection changes and profile changes invalidate stale purpose comparisons.

## Strategy comparison

Profiles now share one owned artifact inventory across independent army strategies. Army manages troop choices, duplicates, favorites, ordering and archived strategies; switching or saving never rewrites shared perk ratings. Each strategy has its own rating overrides and museum layout. The initial unconfigured strategy retains broad factory scoring; configured strategies exclude unselected offensive troop perks, retain defensive/general preferences, and include linked troop families. Explicit strategy overrides, including zero, take precedence. Migrated current armies preserve their previous effective ratings in a legacy snapshot; other converted presets start from clean army defaults. Global/profile inheritance is an explicit choice for migrated strategies because historical army-generated zeros cannot be distinguished from manual preferences. Original storage is retained in a profile migration backup, and full planner backups include strategies and layouts.

The active strategy controls detailed artifact scores and the displayed museum layout. The comparison selection is independent and defaults to all non-archived strategies, including newly added strategies until a subset is selected. Priority shows selectable strategy score columns; Collection adds per-strategy score/rank columns, coverage filters and A/B breakdowns; Active Artifact Totals sums actual unlocked percentages from the selected strategy's layout. Perk ratings exposes local overrides and their inherited sources. Both scanner pages use the same rating resolver. Council priority, Council ratings and Council lineups retain their separate profile-based stat system, with their scope labeled on each page.

Crafting compares each captured item against the two best owned copies of the same hall/category **for every selected strategy**, including unused artifacts and artifacts referenced by other strategy layouts. War Equipment retains four museum slots while crafting uses a two-item collection target. The strategy table separates maximum upgraded score, reroll potential, best owned, second owned, ties, open slots and improvements. Unknown or conflicting fields, incomplete owned stats and uncertain rerolls remain review states; rejection requires complete reliable comparisons for every included strategy. A tie does not displace an owned copy. Keeping saves one shared physical artifact, and updates all strategy benchmarks. Changing strategy while scanning updates scores without reloading or losing the capture. Benchmarks are cached independently per rating resolver/revision and rebuilt when owned data changes.

Crafting category detection reads the coloured label above the benefits panel from the full shared-source preview, separately from the unchanged perk crop. ## OCR during settling, October 18, 2026

Live five-row crafting captures can start one OCR job during the existing 220 ms settling window once the fast OCR worker is ready. Cold startup keeps warming the worker and uses the ordinary settled read if the worker is not ready in time. The same worker processes it; there is no additional worker or job queue. The result cannot update scores, corrections, logs or verified crop evidence before validation. At the ordinary settling point, a fresh observation must exist and every RGBA byte of the frozen panel, prepared category source and original-frame category/Hall/dice crops must match the input an ordinary settled read would consume. Profile, encounter, capture session, calibration and recognition settings must also match. Input witnesses are temporary, limited to 8 MiB each, and released after validation. Oversized or unavailable witnesses use ordinary settled OCR.

Changed pixels, stale frames, hidden or paused capture, replaced artifacts and changed profiles discard early work. A settled retry waits for the existing worker to finish its current bounded OCR call; input rejection retains the warm worker. Unvalidated work cannot populate the verified encounter cache. The original sampling cadence, settling duration, confidence thresholds, conflict handling and uncertain-stat display rules remain in place. Still images and Storage Scanner keep their ordinary read path.

Scores and comparisons publish before constructing editable correction controls, which follow after the first paint. Controls cannot restore a superseded result. Artwork comes from the validated settled source. Log recording and persistence submission remain immediate. The benchmark records the first exact labelled score paint separately from stat availability and full capture, using independent labels only in its observer. `--motion=pixel-change` changes one non-glyph byte after the initial sample; `--motion=flicker` changes then restores it, testing exact rejection and temporal median validation with real OCR.
