==Historical Description==
Some artifacts are never permitted to leave their location of origin, due to their value or historical significance. The Narmer Palette is one such artifact. Discovered in a controlled excavation in Egypt, the palette depicts two different scenes on either face of it, both featuring King Narmer. The location of every piece of the art is incredibly important. At the bottom of the piece there appears to be vanquished enemies, and directly above that is a foe about to be slain by King Narmer. The King’s sandal bearer is the next lowest, followed by King Narmer himself, who is only surpassed by the god Horus, and overlooking everything is the head of the Goddess Bat.

To be considered a boxlock, the weapon must have an internal firing mechanism, typically stored in a box inside the body of the weapon. These internal mechanisms allowed the weapon to be physically smaller compared to other firearm styles such as the sidelock, whose firing mechanism was unsurprisingly stored on the side of the weapon. Actually using a boxlock pistol was sometimes quite difficult, as the hammer of the igniter often sat where aiming sights would otherwise belong.

Napoleon’s three-barreled boxlock pistol was given to him by Lieutenant-Colonel Thomas of the British army, who in his personal life was a dedicated and extravagant hunter. Thomas was so inspired by Napoleon’s victory at the Battle of Marengo, he produced this fabulous three-barrel boxlock pistol, and sent Napoleon a letter, expressing interest in giving the weapon to him. Napoleon graciously accepted, and the two of them would meet several more times to discuss Thomas’ military career and recordings.
